## Toward autonomy and care that makes sense to you

Education comes first. You should understand the changes happening in your brain and body. We give you the tools and coach you through each step, so you can work toward autonomy with the clarity you need to understand your own recovery. We do not rush through symptoms or offer fragmented advice. We slow down and teach you how your brain, body, and daily habits work together.

As a [physiatrist](https://wellnessbyphysiatry.com/services/physiatry-pmr.html) (physical medicine and rehabilitation, PM&R) specialized in [brain injury](https://wellnessbyphysiatry.com/services/brain-injury-rehabilitation.html), I spend time on the things that are important for you, connecting function, safety, and community. I pay close attention to how acquired brain conditions affect thinking, movement, swallowing, behavior, and overall health. I also integrate the six pillars of lifestyle medicine to help you make choices that support long-lasting results. The pillars refer to basic functions such as sleep, nutrition, keeping stress levels in check, regular exercise, and minimizing the use of risky substances such as recreational drugs and alcoholic beverages.

By blending [evidence-based rehabilitation](https://wellnessbyphysiatry.com/services.html) with practical [lifestyle medicine](https://wellnessbyphysiatry.com/services/lifestyle-medicine.html) guidance, we deliver compassionate, personalized care. You will know what we are doing and why, and you will feel supported. The goal is care that helps you get better and understand how to stay better.

### Education comes first

Good decisions about your recovery start with understanding what is happening to you. After a [traumatic brain injury, stroke, or other acquired brain condition](https://wellnessbyphysiatry.com/services/brain-injury-rehabilitation.html), or [concussion](https://wellnessbyphysiatry.com/services/concussion-management.html), [Dr. Kopp](https://wellnessbyphysiatry.com/team.html) explains the changes in your thinking, movement, mood, and energy in plain language, as many times as it takes, and teaches you how your brain, body, and daily habits work together. That understanding turns a care plan from something done to you into something you can carry forward on your own, with your family and caregivers beside you.

### Care that connects the whole picture

Dr. Kopp is a [physiatrist](https://wellnessbyphysiatry.com/services/physiatry-pmr.html) who specializes in brain injury rehabilitation medicine, so she looks at function, safety, and community together. She pays close attention to how acquired brain conditions, including traumatic brain injury, stroke, dementia, encephalopathy, diffuse axonal injury, and anoxic brain injury, affect thinking, movement, swallowing, behavior, and overall health status. You see the same physician at every visit, at our East Loop or Lincoln Park [location in Chicago](https://wellnessbyphysiatry.com/contact.html), and she coordinates the rest of your care so it stays connected.

### Rebuilding strength, restoring function

We combine evidence-based rehabilitation care plans with practical [lifestyle medicine](https://wellnessbyphysiatry.com/services/lifestyle-medicine.html) guidance, including health coaching and group sessions in English, Español, and Português. When hands-on therapy is the right next step, such as physical, occupational, or speech therapy, we refer you to [trusted partners](https://wellnessbyphysiatry.com/referrals.html) and stay involved in your care. The result is compassionate, personalized care that helps you get better and understand how to stay better.

## The pillars we build habits on

The pillars refer to basic functions that shape how well you recover.

- **Sleep** restorative rest, treated as part of your care plan rather than an afterthought.
- **Nutrition** what you eat, adjusted to your own overall health status and goals.
- **Stress** keeping stress levels in check with practical, repeatable habits.
- **Exercise** regular movement, paced to where you are in your recovery.
- **Risky substances** minimizing the use of substances that work against your recovery, talked through without judgment.
- **Social connection** staying connected to people who understand what you are going through.
